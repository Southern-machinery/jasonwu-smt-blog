Watch a manual wave soldering line for ten minutes and you will see the real cost driver: people walking. An operator carries a pallet of boards to the machine, waits, unloads, carries the empty pallet back, then walks for more boards. The furnace is never the bottleneck of a manual wave line — the feet are. Our answer has been embarrassingly simple: a wireless return trolley that does the walking.

## The arithmetic I use before recommending any AGV

The SAGV30C-class cart we sell costs about **US$2,500 — roughly two months of one operator's wage** in most of the markets we serve. That anchor is deliberate. Before any automation conversation, I ask three questions:

1. **How many kilometers does that operator walk per shift?** Pallet runs on a wave line routinely eat 5–8 km of walking. That is the job description nobody wrote.
2. **What breaks when they stop walking?** On most lines: nothing. The pallets still arrive, faster and on schedule.
3. **What is the alternative capital?** Rebuilding the line into a rotary conveyor system costs an order of magnitude more and stops production for weeks. The trolley solution is designed to retrofit a running line in a lunch break, not a shutdown.

If your answers make the cart replace one labor position — which is the design intent, one unit per one walking job — payback is measured in months, and the [output effect our AGV catalog documents](https://file.autoinsertion.com/public/Southern%20Machinery%20Product/AGV%20Southern%20Machinery.pdf) (we have measured runs showing 10%+ throughput on constrained wave lines) is a bonus, not the promise.

## What the machine actually does

The SFY03/SAGV family runs wireless on rechargeable battery, follows a set route between the loader, furnace, and unloader positions, and returns empty pallets on its own. ## When an AGV does NOT earn its keep

I tell customers no more often than you would expect:

- **One-off moves.** If pallets travel twice an hour, a person is cheaper. AGVs win on rhythm.
- **Mazes.** The line must have a repeatable route. If your layout changes monthly, fix the layout first.
- **Expecting headcount deletion on day one.** The honest first win is removing the walking from the job, then reassigning the person to inspection or kitting. The wage math still works; the org chart just needs honesty.

## The loop this starts

Once one trolley is accepted on the floor, the same wireless logic spreads: magazine racks to loaders, loaders to furnaces, furnaces to depaneling. That is how a manual line becomes a smart line without a demolition budget — one route at a time.
